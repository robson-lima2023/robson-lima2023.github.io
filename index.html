About

Forest science, Amazonian ecology, and quantitative methods

I am a forest scientist and professor working at the interface between forest mensuration, forest biometrics, tropical forest ecology, remote sensing, spatial modeling, and biodiversity conservation.

My research focuses on understanding forest structure, tree diversity, biomass, giant trees, and species distributions across tropical landscapes, with emphasis on the Amazon.

Research

Main research areas

Amazonian Giant Trees

Ecology, distribution, monitoring, and conservation of giant trees in Amazonian forests.

LiDAR Remote Sensing

Mapping canopy height, forest structure, biomass, and spatial patterns using airborne LiDAR.

Species Distribution Modeling

Modeling current and future species distributions under environmental and climate change.

Forest Mensuration

Forest biometrics, volume, biomass, growth, yield, structure, and forest management.

Machine Learning

Predictive modeling using Random Forest, BRT, Maxent, GLM, GAM, and ensemble approaches.

Climate Change and Conservation

Assessing forest vulnerability, biodiversity patterns, and conservation priorities.

Projects

Featured projects

Integrated Monitoring of Amazonian Giant Trees

Long-term ecological research focused on giant trees, forest dynamics, LiDAR, biomass, soils, microclimate, and biodiversity in eastern Amazonia.

LiDAR-based Mapping of Giant Trees in the Amazon

Mapping and modeling the density, distribution, and environmental drivers of exceptionally tall trees across Amazonian landscapes.

Species Distribution Modeling in R

Development of scientific and educational resources on SDM using real data, ecological theory, spatial predictors, and multiple modeling algorithms.
